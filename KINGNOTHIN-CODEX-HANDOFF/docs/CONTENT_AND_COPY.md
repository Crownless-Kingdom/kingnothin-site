# Content Voice and Protected Copy

## Voice

Write with seriousness, urgency, clarity, and occasional dry wit. Treat the reader as intelligent but not necessarily technical. Define unfamiliar terms when they first matter. Explain the mechanism beneath the headline and connect abstract technology to daily life.

Prefer:

- Concrete actors, actions, incentives, and consequences.
- Short, controlled sentences around important ideas.
- Evidence before certainty.
- Honest limits and meaningful choices.
- Invitations to regain agency instead of commands designed to frighten.

Avoid:

- Corporate filler and motivational fog.
- Treating readers as helpless or foolish.
- Overheated dystopian claims unsupported by evidence.
- Dense jargon, unearned prophecy, or piles of fragmented slogans.

## Research and factual writing

- Distinguish documented fact, interpretation, prediction, and creative speculation.
- For current or consequential claims, use primary sources where practical and cite links near the relevant claim.
- Do not manufacture statistics or citations.
- When sources conflict, explain the disagreement plainly.
- Monetized content must disclose meaningful commercial relationships and must not undermine reader sovereignty.
